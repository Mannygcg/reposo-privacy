Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: October 25, 2026

Reposo helps you put your phone down. It blocks the apps, websites and notifications you choose while a lock is running, and every quarter-hour you stay away adds a fish to your net in a small Sea of Galilee fishing game. This policy explains, plainly, what the app does and doesn't do with your information.

In short

Reposo collects no personal data, and has no way to. The Android app does not request access to the internet, so there is nothing it could send anywhere, even if it tried. The permissions it asks for are used only on your phone, only to block what you chose to block.

Which apps you use, what you block and how long you stay locked never leave your device.

Information we collect

None. Reposo does not collect, transmit, or have access to any personal data, device data, usage analytics, or advertising identifiers. The app includes no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting software of any kind, and there is no account to create.

This isn't just a promise, it's a technical fact. Reposo's Android app manifest does not declare the INTERNET permission, which means the operating system does not allow the app to open a network connection at all. There is no server Reposo talks to, because it was never built to talk to one.

Permissions and why

Every permission below is optional to grant, explained in the app before you're asked, and used only on your phone.

  • Accessibility service: this is how Reposo blocks. While it is on, Reposo notices which app is in the foreground and, in supported browsers (such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi and Samsung Internet), the address shown in the address bar. It compares these with the apps and websites you chose to block and, when they match during a lock, covers the screen with Reposo's lock screen. If you turn on strict mode, it also recognises the Settings and uninstall screens that mention Reposo, so they can't be used to get around a lock. Reposo does not record what you type, read other content on your screen, or keep a history of the apps or sites you visit.
  • Notification access: while a lock is running, Reposo hides notifications from the apps you blocked and keeps a copy of each one (the app, its title and text, and when it arrived) so it can show you a "While you were locked" summary afterwards. Notifications from apps you didn't block are left alone. These copies stay on your phone, are capped at the latest 200, and are deleted when you tap Clear on the summary or start your next lock.
  • Usage access: if you allow it, setup reads your phone's own app-usage totals for the last week to suggest the apps you use most as ones to block. This is used for the suggestion and is not saved.
  • NFC: to read and, if you ask, write a Reposo link to an NFC tag, so tapping the tag can lock or unlock. Reposo saves a one-way hash of the tag's hardware ID to recognise it later.
  • Camera: only while you are scanning a Reposo QR code. Images are processed on the device to read the code and are never saved.
  • Notifications: to show a status notification while you're locked and for how long.
  • Vibration: for a short buzz when a tag or code is recognised.

Reposo also reads the list of apps installed on your phone so you can pick which ones to block. That list is used on screen and is not sent anywhere.

What's stored on your device

To work, Reposo saves a small amount of information in Android's private app storage. This information never leaves your device and is never seen by us:

  • Your modes: the apps and websites each one blocks, its message, and its options
  • Your tags and QR codes: their names and the identifiers Reposo uses to recognise them
  • Your schedules, and settings such as break length, emergency unlocks and mindful pauses
  • The current lock, if one is running, and a history of past locks (when, how long, how they ended)
  • Notifications held during the current or last lock, as described above
  • Your fishing progress: fish caught, badges earned, and how your fisher looks

This data is sandboxed to Reposo by the operating system itself; other apps cannot read it, and none of it is ever sent to us, because we don't operate any server.

If you have turned on Android's own device backup (for example, backup to your Google account), Android may include Reposo's data in that backup, so it can be restored if you reinstall the app or move to a new phone. That backup is made and kept by Android under your account; Reposo never sees it, and you can turn it off in your phone's settings. Otherwise, uninstalling Reposo deletes this information permanently.

Data sharing & third parties

Reposo does not share data with anyone, for a simple reason: it does not collect any data to share. The app has no third-party integrations, no advertising network, no analytics provider and no cloud backend.

If you share or print a QR code, Reposo hands the image to the app or printer you pick, using Android's standard share and print screens. What happens next is up to you and that app.

Children's privacy

Reposo does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, of any age, including children. Because the app has no data collection mechanism at all, this applies to every user regardless of age.

Data security

Since no information is transmitted off your device, there is no data in transit to secure and no server-side storage to protect. The information described above is kept using the same operating-system protections Android applies to every app's private storage.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, for example if a future version of Reposo adds a feature that does involve network access, it will be updated on this same page with a revised "last updated" date above. We will not retroactively change what this policy says about a past version of the app.
